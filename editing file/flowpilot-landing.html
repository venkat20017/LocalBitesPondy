The Daily Reality

Recognize your Monday morning?

This isn't a strategy problem. It's 50 repetitive decisions your team makes before 10am.

01

3–4 duplicate tickets. Every. Single. Morning.

The same network outage gets reported by every affected team. Your engineer manually reads all four, confirms they're duplicates, merges the context, and closes three. That's 25 minutes before any real work starts.

"We are able to close the second one and mark as a duplicate, but this is a bit of a manual thing." — r/sysadmin
02

Writing 2-paragraph status updates on tickets that haven't moved.

Leadership wants 3–4 updates per high-priority ticket per day. Your engineer copy-pastes progress notes, rewrites the same context, and tracks down the same status — while the actual problem waits.

"They want 3–4 updates... minimum required amount for a comment is 2 paragraphs." — r/sysadmin
03

SLA breach reports your CTO pulls before you do.

Pulling SLA data requires filters, exports, and manual cross-referencing. By the time you have the number, someone upstream already has it too — and now you're explaining, not reporting.

"Pulling SLA breach data requires extra filters and exports." — G2, Jira Service Management
